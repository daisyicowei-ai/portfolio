♡ Early career / Feature design

Hinge: Feature Design

Trivia Match Feature: Designing a Fun, Consent-Forward Alternative to the Rose

✦

A university concept exploring how a little play can make starting a conversation feel more mutual and less pressured.

01 · The product

About Hinge

Hinge is a dating app built to be deleted, focused on meaningful connections through prompts, curated matches, and features like “Standouts” and “Roses” to highlight high-interest profiles.

02 · The problem

A high-pressure way to say hello

Hinge’s Standouts feature puts highly desirable profiles behind a paywall. To engage, users must send a paid Rose, a bold gesture many find uncomfortable, awkward, or overly forward.

“

“Too intense” or “creepy”

Women describing the Rose
“

“Awkward” and not worth paying for

Men describing the Rose
↘

Skipped Standouts altogether

Even when profiles appealed

This resulted in low conversion, frustration, and missed matches, especially when the app’s own algorithm put top matches behind a wall users didn’t want to cross.

03 · The goal

Make connection feel more mutual

Reimagine Hinge’s monetization within Standouts by designing a more fun, low-pressure way to connect, without sacrificing revenue or consent.
  • Increase engagement with Standouts
  • Encourage connection without making users uncomfortable
  • Support Hinge’s existing paywall model
  • Create natural conversation starters post-match

04 · The solution

Trivia Request

Introduce an interactive game layer: a trivia challenge users can request instead of sending a Rose.

01

Choose to play

Users can request a trivia game instead of paying for a Rose.

02

Pick a theme

The sender chooses a theme, such as pop culture, dating, or film.

03

Take turns

The match receives “Want to play a trivia round?” Players answer one question each, turn-by-turn, like iMessage games.

Every outcome opens a door

＝

Tie

A mutual win and a chance to open a conversation.

✦

One winner

Playful tension and a built-in conversation starter.

Win or lose, users are more likely to message — and it feels mutual and respectful.

05 · My contribution

My Role & Deliverables

As lead concept designer on this university project, I owned the entire UX process.

⌕

User research & interviews

Identified core hesitation patterns with Roses and ran 1:1s and surveys with Hinge users to validate behavior.

✳

Feature concept & game logic

Designed the core interaction flow for trivia and balanced fun with just enough friction to keep it light but valuable.

⌘

User flows & wireframes

Created end-to-end screens, from sending a request to making a match.

▦

Design system additions

Created new components, including question cards and countdown timers.

✓

Usability testing

Tested a mid-fidelity prototype with users to refine pacing and clarity.

06 · The impact

Why It Works

01

Reduces pressure

Lowers the emotional stakes of initiating.

02

Increases conversion

Users may be more willing to “play” than pay immediately.

03

Keeps monetization

The feature could be bundled in Hinge’s premium tier.

04

Enhances connection

The game becomes an instant conversation starter.

07 · Looking back

Reflection

This project taught me how to:

  • Balance business needs with human comfort
  • Use gamification to support consent, not bypass it
  • Design systems that encourage action without being manipulative
  • Build a feature that’s fun, scalable, and rooted in real behavior
