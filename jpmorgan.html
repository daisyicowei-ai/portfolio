01 / The problem

Two platforms. One fragmented experience.

Clients relied on two separate reporting platforms: a decades-old legacy system and a newer platform with a different structure.

  • Neither offered a consistent, intuitive workflow.
  • Clients needed service teams for routine tasks like running a report or setting a schedule.
  • Switching between platforms added friction and made the experience harder to learn.

The split increased operational load on internal teams, created friction for clients, and steepened onboarding for new users.

More support for routine work Fragmented client workflows Steeper learning curve

02 / The opportunity

Make self-service feel possible.

Build a modern reporting experience that would:

  • Let clients run and customize reports on their own.
  • Reduce the load on service teams.
  • Improve usability without compromising data accuracy or security.

A clear design goal, grounded in client needs and platform trust.

05 / How I approached it

Make powerful tools feel manageable.

01

Designed the most complex flow: report creation.

Clients needed to build reports from scratch: add and remove attributes, arrange the layout, and add calculations. I kept each step manageable and made changes give clear feedback, so clients could tell their report was valid as they built it.

Live preview · Validation states · Grouped attributes · Undo actions
02

Balanced control with safety.

Sensible defaults and guardrails made the safe path the fastest one, while templates, validation, and progressive disclosure let clients go deeper when needed.

Templates · Validation · Progressive disclosure
03

Sequenced complexity instead of hiding it.

Scheduling used to put every parameter on one screen. I broke it into steps where each decision set up the next, reducing abandoned schedules and repeat support requests.

One clear decision at a time
